SERIES STYLE — every hero image is one more page from the same illustrated series. Same artist, same ink, same palette, same lettering. Only the scene changes.

MEDIUM
Editorial comic-book illustration. Clean, confident dark ink outlines of even weight around every figure and object. Flat colour fills with simple two-tone cel shading (one base tone, one shadow tone) — no gradients, no painterly brushwork, no texture. Crisp edges, generous negative space, calm balanced composition. Not a photograph, not a 3D render, not watercolour, not a sketch.

PALETTE (use these and little else)
- Deep navy #1A4480 — anchor colour for suits, screens, chart bars, and the title lettering
- Teal #2E7D8C — secondary accent for blazers, badges, chart segments
- Warm amber #E8A33D — sparing highlight colour: charts, icons, a lanyard, a coin
- Cream #F5F1E8 — backgrounds, walls, the title band
- Slate blue-grey #7A8FA6 and soft sky blue #C9DAE8 — rooms, chairs, glass, backgrounds
- Natural skin tones across a diverse cast; warm brown for hair and wood
Muted and professional throughout. No neon, no saturated reds or purples, no black backgrounds.

CAST
Business professionals in modern attire: blazers, shirts, lanyards, glasses, laptops. Simplified, friendly, readable faces with drawn eyes and clear expressions (curious, focused, sceptical, delighted). A mix of ages, genders, and ethnicities in every crowd. People are the focal point; they are mid-action — presenting, listening, taking notes, pointing, comparing, deciding.

WORLD
Conference stages, auditoriums, breakout rooms, boardrooms, open-plan offices, hotel lobbies, backstage. Props that carry ideas visually: presentation screens, dashboards with bar and line charts, laptops, notebooks, name badges, calendars, thought bubbles with simple icons. Large windows with a soft city skyline are welcome.

PAGE FORMAT (the series signature)
Wide 16:9. A cream (#F5F1E8) band across the top of the image carries the post title in bold navy (#1A4480) sans-serif lettering, clearly legible and well-kerned, like a page header. The illustrated scene fills the rest of the frame beneath it. Any other text in the scene (slide headings, chart labels, badge names) must be tiny and abstract — short strokes or a one-word label at most — never a readable sentence.

WHAT VARIES FROM PAGE TO PAGE
The scene, the setting, the number of people, the camera angle (wide stage shot, over-the-shoulder at a table, medium shot of two people talking, a crowd from the side), and the props. Match the scene to the specific subject of the article. Do not repeat the same layout two pages in a row.

WHAT NEVER VARIES
The ink outlines, the flat cel shading, the palette above, the cream title band with navy lettering, the friendly professional cast, and the 16:9 frame.

AVOID
Photorealism, 3D renders, painterly or watercolour styles, rough sketch lines, heavy gradients, lens flares, dramatic high-contrast lighting, neon, glitch effects, uncanny close-up portrait faces, generic handshake stock compositions, readable body text anywhere in the artwork, and copying the reference image's exact scene, people, or layout.
